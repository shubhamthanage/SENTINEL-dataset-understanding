1Dataset overview

Purpose and business problem

PayVista is an RBI-authorised payment aggregator processing about 4.2 million transactions a day. Over the last 18 months its fraud losses rose from 4.1 to 9.8 basis points. Its 340 static rules decline or review 2.3% of transactions, yet only 1 in 19 of those is fraud. Blocked customers get no explanation.

SENTINEL replaces that with an in-house LLM. For every transaction it returns three things within 250 ms at p99: a fraud probability, an ALLOW / REVIEW / BLOCK decision, and a plain-language reason.

This dataset is a synthetic stand-in for PayVista's real data. It has the same fields, the same 0.6% fraud rate and the same payment mix, at a smaller scale. It is meant for designing and testing the full pipeline: features, labels, training corpora, rules and audit. It cannot establish real model accuracy.

What one record represents

The core record is one authorisation attempt: a payer (customer) tries to pay a merchant at a specific moment, through a channel (UPI, card, wallet or net banking), possibly from a known device. This includes attempts that later fail, so it is an authorisation history, not a settlement ledger.

Around that record sit:

  • what happened to the payment (outcome)
  • whether it was later confirmed as fraud (label, available 60 days later)
  • the engineered risk signals known at that instant (features)
  • what the system decided (decision audit)
  • any investigator note or chargeback narrative written about it

Types of data available

Structured / tabular

Transactions, customers, merchants, devices, outcomes, labels, features, decisions, rules and thresholds. Columns are integers, decimals, booleans, categoricals, timestamps and hashed IDs.

Semi-structured (JSON)

Locations ({"city","lat","lon"}), reason-code arrays, rule expressions, model request and feature vectors, and document metadata.

Unstructured text

Investigator notes, chargeback narratives, playbooks, regulatory teaching exercises, guidelines and serialised transaction histories, plus LLM training files (CPT, SFT, gold, DPO).

3Business mapping

Why a real organisation collects this data

  • To stop losses: chargebacks and write-offs hit ₹3.6 crore last quarter. Each confirmed fraud label is money the business either lost or saved.
  • To stop blocking good customers: every false decline is a lost sale and an unhappy merchant. The labels and decisions show how often the current system gets it wrong.
  • For regulation and audit: RBI, PCI DSS and the DPDP Act expect PayVista to be able to show why it blocked a payment and who changed a rule. That is what decision_audit and config_changes record.
  • To reuse its own knowledge: five years of investigator notes and chargeback narratives are expertise no rules engine can use.

Insights, patterns and anomalies we found

SignalFraud rate when trueFraud rate otherwiseLift
Night (00:00–05:00 IST)2.72%0.25%≈ 11×
Payment attempt failed3.13%0.29%≈ 11×
Device shared by several payers3.60%0.40%≈ 9×
4 risk flags together vs none4.48%0.12%≈ 36×
Payment channel (any)≈ 0.60% on every channelnone
  • This matches what the investigators told us in CRD 3.3: amount alone is weak, but context is strong. The median fraud amount is ₹1,498 against ₹1,250 for genuine payments, almost the same. Combine night + failures + new device and the risk rises sharply.
  • Several of the most-shared devices (up to 108 payers each) are on the blocklist. Mule and shared-device networks are a real pattern here.
  • Fraud is a rare event: 1 in about 167 transactions. Accuracy is meaningless as a metric, so PR-AUC and recall are the right measures.

Better decisions this enables

  • Tune ALLOW / REVIEW / BLOCK thresholds by measuring the false-decline cost against the fraud cost.
  • Send the review team fewer and better cases, each with its reasons attached.
  • Give support staff a sentence to tell the customer instead of "declined".
  • Change policy in minutes through versioned thresholds and lists instead of 10–15 day rule releases.

Attributes that matter most

The behavioural and context signals: is_night / hour_of_day, failed_transactions / has_recent_failures, is_new_device, distinct_customers_per_device_7d, amount_to_avg_ratio, is_new_city / distance, account_age, velocity (txn_count_last_1h, txn_amount_last_24h_paise), combined_risk_flags, and the target fraud_label.

For the LLM, the serialised history plus investigator notes matter as much as any single column.

4Data quality observations

We profiled all rows (not a sample) with DuckDB. Severity: HIGH affects the model or design, MEDIUM needs handling, OK is verified clean.

Missing values carry almost no fraud signal (0.66% vs 0.60% for missing device). A flag plus a neutral default is safe, and dropping the rows isn't needed.

Duplicates

  • OK No duplicate keys in any table. No duplicate natural keys (customer + merchant + amount + time). No duplicate document hashes.
  • MEDIUM Template repetition in the text. The 39,006 documents reduce to 30,902 template fingerprints. The 8,500 gold examples have only 59 distinct targets, and all DPO pairs share one rejection reason. Near-duplicate removal by meaning is "not certified". The LLM could learn to copy templates instead of reasoning.
  • MEDIUM Stray extracted files. Some parts were unzipped next to their originals (e.g. customers/all/part-00003.csv/, devices/all/part-00021.csv.zip). A loader using *.csv* would load those rows twice. Load only *.csv.gz and check against manifest.json.

Data types and consistency

  • OK amount_paise equals transaction_amount × 100 in every row. Paise (integer) is the authoritative value, which avoids rounding errors.
  • MEDIUM Timezone. The CRD describes timestamps as IST, but they are stored in UTC. Night flags, splits and account creation (18:30 UTC = IST midnight) all depend on converting correctly. Do this in one shared function.
  • MEDIUM Locations, reason codes and rule expressions are JSON stored as text in the CSVs. They need a fixed schema and parsing.
  • MEDIUM Window definitions live in different places. previous_transactions (30 days), average_transaction_value (90 days) and failed_transactions (24 h) are defined only in the CRD, not in feature_catalogue.json. Their windows differ, so a row can have previous_transactions = 0 and still have an average, which is correct. A pipeline that recomputes counts from full history would disagree with 92% of rows. Write all windows into one feature spec.
  • MEDIUM customer_age is copied from the customer table and never changes over 17 months.

Outliers and unusual values

  • MEDIUM Amounts: median ₹1,251, p99 ₹25,474, maximum ₹79.9 lakh. 349 rows are above ₹10 lakh. Use a log scale; don't delete them, because the CRD calls them rare but real.
  • HIGH UPI is unrealistic: 830 UPI payments are above ₹1 lakh and 205 above ₹10 lakh, beyond normal UPI per-transaction limits. Add channel-specific validation.
  • MEDIUM amount_to_avg_ratio reaches 32,318 (284 rows above 1,000×). Cap or log it.
  • SIGNAL 120 devices are used by more than one payer, the top ones by about 100 payers. These are fraud rings or mules, not noise. Keep them.
  • HIGH Time artefact: there are zero transactions between 05:00 and 05:59 IST, and night hours carry about 60% of daytime volume. Real traffic will look different, so time-based patterns may not carry over to production.

Leakage risks HIGH

These columns must never be model inputs:

  • reviewer_feedback.verdict, which agrees with the label in 100% of 19,376 rows.
  • decision_audit probability, decision and risk level.
  • The current transaction's payment_outcomes.status.
  • label_source, label_available_at and eligible_at_next_split_start.
  • split, IDs, and the scenario IDs in ground_truth.

Class imbalance and label maturity HIGH

Only 0.6% of rows are fraud. Of the 144,803 validation rows, only 2,903 (26 frauds) have mature labels before the holdout begins. Probabilities can't be calibrated on 26 positives, so calibration needs a longer history window.

Privacy and security

  • OK Every row is synthetic, IDs are hashed, and merchant names are placeholders.
  • OK We scanned all 39,006 documents (the shipped audit covered only 1,000). There were 0 matches for email, Indian mobile numbers, PAN, Aadhaar, card numbers, UPI VPAs or raw customer/device IDs.
  • MEDIUM decision_audit.request stores customer ID, device ID and exact location. In production these are pseudonymous personal data under the DPDP Act 2023. They need masking, role-based access and a retention period.
  • MEDIUM Open gaps:
    • No human PII audit has been done.
    • The regulatory documents are invented teaching scenarios, not real RBI/NPCI circulars.
    • The Supabase demo doesn't provide the CRD's AWS data residency or immutable (Object Lock) audit storage.
    • Data must never leave PayVista's account, which rules out third-party AI APIs.
Impact summary.
  • Design: paise-only money, one IST conversion, typed JSON, a .gz-only loader checked against the manifest, and a masked, access-controlled audit store.
  • Training: drop the leakage columns, scale outliers, handle the imbalance, calibrate on more history, and deduplicate templates.
  • Analytics: don't quote volume, hourly trends, scores or latencies, which are all simulated.

5AI/ML opportunities

Prediction targets
  • fraud_label: main binary target
  • Decision (ALLOW / REVIEW / BLOCK): derived from the probability and thresholds
  • Reason codes + explanation: generated JSON output
Model inputs
  • Transaction: amount, channel, category, time, account age, 30-day count, 90-day average, 24-h failures, customer age
  • All FE-01…FE-10 features plus the missingness flags
  • Serialised 20-event history and retrieved investigator knowledge (for the LLM)
Never inputs
  • IDs, split, label metadata, reviewer verdicts, simulated decisions and scores, current outcome status, scenario IDs

Preprocessing steps

  1. Load only .csv.gz or Parquet files; check row counts and SHA values against manifest.json.
  2. Convert UTC to IST in one place; parse JSON columns into typed fields.
  3. Use paise everywhere; apply log1p to amount, the ratio and 24-h totals, and cap extreme ratios.
  4. Keep nulls as nulls and add missingness flags; never treat unknown as new.
  5. Split by time only (never randomly); respect the 60-day label delay; filter eligible_at_next_split_start for strict backtests.
  6. Handle the 0.6% imbalance with class weights or focal loss; keep negative downsampling on the training data only.
  7. Serialise each transaction and its history into the fixed fmt-v1 prompt within the 1,024-token budget.

Feature engineering opportunities

  • Behaviour versus the payer's own normal: amount z-score, usual hours, usual merchants and cities.
  • Velocity: attempts in 10 min, 1 h and 24 h; failure-then-success bursts.
  • Graph features (Phase 2 in the CRD): device ↔ customer links, shared-device clusters and ring detection.
  • Interactions: night × new device × failures, matching the investigator's "₹50,000 at 3 AM" example.
  • Merchant risk over time: rolling category and merchant fraud rates using only mature labels.
  • Time since the last transaction and since the last device or city change.

Real-time vs batch

Real-time (per authorisation, ≤ 250 ms)
  • Online feature store holds velocity counters, last-90-day device and city sets, and the 24-h failure count.
  • Features are read before the current event is applied, exactly as feature_as_of shows.
  • Model score → threshold → hard rules → decision → audit log row.
  • If the model is down, the rules act as the fallback.
Batch (daily, weekly, monthly)
  • Label maturation after 60 days; monthly refresh of the MCC encoder.
  • Retraining with reviewer feedback; drift monitoring on feature distributions.
  • Threshold tuning and back-testing; corpus deduplication and embedding generation.
  • Dashboards: fraud bps, false-decline rate, review-queue size.

6Discussion notes

Key observations

  • The dataset matches the CRD's shape: payment mix 58 / 31 / 7 / 4, a 0.6% fraud rate, 3% missing location, UPI-only missing devices and rare large amounts.
  • Context beats amount: night, recent failures and shared devices each raise the fraud rate about 10×.
  • The data is built to prevent leakage: point-in-time features, a frozen encoder, a 60-day label delay and a fenced holdout. All ten built-in checks pass.
  • The text corpus is large but repetitive, and nothing in it has been reviewed by a human.

Decisions taken by the pod

  • Paise is the single money field, and all time logic runs in IST.
  • Splits are by time only, and the holdout is never touched before acceptance.
  • Nulls stay null and get flags; extreme amounts are kept and log-scaled.
  • The leakage columns listed in Section 4 are excluded from every model input.
  • PR-AUC and recall are the primary metrics; accuracy is not reported.

Assumptions

  • The synthetic distributions are close enough to production for design work, but not for accuracy claims.
  • Labels are final 60 days after the transaction, and a label of 0 means "mature, no fraud reported".
  • Hashed IDs are stable over time (the same payer always gets the same hash).

Open questions for the client

  1. Are the previous_transactions (30 days) and average_transaction_value (90 days) windows final? Can they be added to the feature catalogue?
  2. What are the per-channel amount limits, especially for UPI above ₹1 lakh?
  3. Can the calibration window be extended, given only 26 mature fraud cases in validation?
  4. Will real RBI/NPCI circulars and human-reviewed gold and DPO examples be provided?
  5. What is the retention period and the access policy for audit logs that hold IDs and location?
  6. Is the missing 05:00 hour a generator artefact, or a real maintenance window?

Learnings

  • Understanding when each value becomes known matters as much as knowing what it means. Most of the leakage traps here are about time.
  • A column can be perfectly predictive and still be useless. The reviewer verdict is the label in disguise.
  • Missing data isn't automatically bad. Here it is uninformative, and the flags handle it.
  • Business context (CRD 3.3) told us which features to look at before the numbers confirmed it.
